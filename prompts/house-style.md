## House style (applies to every book)

- Use an em dash (—) only for speech that is cut off, placed immediately before the closing quotation mark, as in "I'll—" or "Wait, that's not—". Nowhere else: not for asides, pauses, interruptions in narration, or joining clauses.
- Where you might reach for a dash, use a comma, a period, a semicolon, a colon, or parentheses instead. Often the cleanest fix is two sentences.
- Never use an en dash (–) or a spaced hyphen ( - ) as a dash either.
- Hyphens inside compound words are fine: light-spell, well-kept, half-drowned.
